# Typefaces for easier reading

Three typefaces made to make reading easier, and a way to lead your eye along the line. They work in e-books, text and Markdown files.

## Choose a typeface

1. Open a book.
2. Click **Aa** (Text size and typeface) in the book's bar. A PDF keeps its own letters, so it has no **Aa**.
3. Under **Typeface**, choose:

| Typeface | Made for |
| --- | --- |
| **OpenDyslexic (dyslexia)** | Readers with dyslexia: letters with heavier bottoms, so they do not seem to turn round or swap |
| **Atkinson Hyperlegible (low vision)** | Readers with low vision: from the Braille Institute, each letter is easy to tell from the others |
| **Lexend (easier flow)** | Anyone: wider letters and spacing, designed to make reading more fluent |

Your choice applies to every book and is kept. To go back, choose **The book's own**.

## Bold word starts

**Bold word starts** sets the first letters of each word in bold. Your eye follows the bold parts and your mind fills in the rest, which many readers find faster and easier to keep their place with (the idea is known as Bionic Reading).

1. Click **Aa** (Text size and typeface) in the book's bar.
2. Tick **Bold word starts**.

Untick it to go back. It works together with any typeface.

::: tip Where they come from
The typefaces come from [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org) under the SIL Open Font License, and are part of the app, so they work offline. Bold word starts uses [text-vide](https://github.com/Gumball12/text-vide).
:::
