# Speed reading

Speed reading shows the book one word at a time, always in the same spot, so your eyes stay still and you read faster without losing your place. Each word is lined up on its focus letter, shown in colour, which is where the eye recognises a word best.

## Start

1. Open a book and turn to the page you want to start from.
2. Click **Study**, then **Speed reading**. (Or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>K</kbd> and type **Speed read**.)
3. Tap the word area, or **▶**, to start.

The words come from the page you are on to the end of the book.

## Control it

| To | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Pause or go on | Tap the word area, or press <kbd>Space</kbd> |
| Go back or on a word | **⏮** and **⏭**, or <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> |
| Read faster or slower | Move the **words a minute** slider (100 to 900) |
| Stop | **✕**, or <kbd>Esc</kbd> |

The pace you choose is kept for next time. Longer words and the ends of sentences stay a little longer on screen. The bar under the word shows how far you are, and the line below it how long the rest of the book takes at this pace.

Start around 250 to 300 words a minute and go up a little at a time. For study material you need to remember, read normally, and use speed reading for a first pass or for revision.

::: tip Where it comes from
Speed reading uses [rsvp-engine](https://github.com/altunmustafa/rsvp-engine), an open source engine for this way of reading (called RSVP).
:::
