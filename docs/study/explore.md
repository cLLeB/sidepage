# Explore a book

See a book from above: who is in it, how its mood rises and falls, the phrases it keeps saying, the places it goes, and how it is written. Click **Study**, then **Explore this book**. Everything is worked out on your device from the book's words; only the map asks the internet.

## People

A map of the people the book names, each sized by how often they come, joined to the people they come with (named in the same passages), the line thicker the more often. Click a name to see how often it comes, a strip showing where in the book it does (click a mark to go there), and who it comes with most.

Names are found by their capitals: a word written with a capital in the middle of sentences, and never in small letters. "Mr Darcy" and "Darcy" count as one person.

## Mood

The book's emotional arc, from its first part to its last: above the line, the words of that part are mostly warm and hopeful; below it, mostly dark or sad (the AFINN word list). **Brightest** and **Darkest** go to the highest and lowest points. It works for books in English.

## Phrases

Runs of three to six words the book says three times or more: refrains, set phrases, an author's habits, the names of things it keeps coming back to. Click one to see each place it comes, with the words around it.

## Places

The places the book names after "in", "at", "to" or "from", on a map (OpenStreetMap). Each dot is sized by how often the place comes; click one to go to where the book first names it. Finding them on the map asks OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service once for each place, then remembers it on your device.

## Style

How the book is written, next to the other books you opened: its length and reading time, words per sentence, variety of words, share of rare words, and school grade (Flesch Kincaid).

## Listen

Make a sound file of the pages to listen to away from the screen, on a walk or on the bus.

1. Under **What**, choose this page, or this page and some after it.
2. Under **How**, choose **Two voices talk it over** (one voice asks what the part is about, what its key terms mean, and a question to test yourself; the other answers from the book) or **Read as it is**.
3. Click **Make the sound**, listen, and click **Save as MP3** to keep it.

It uses the natural voices, on your device. The first time, they are fetched (about 60 MB each).
