# Study methods

Well tried ways of learning, each worked on the book you are reading. Click **Study**, then **Study methods**: the panel opens beside the book, with a tab for each.

## Memorise a passage

For a definition, a law, a quotation, lines of a poem or a script.

1. Select the passage on the page and click **Use the selected passage** (or pick one of your highlights).
2. Read it aloud. Click **More gaps**: a few of its words are hidden. Read it again, filling the gaps from memory. Tap a gap to see its word.
3. Go on with **More gaps**: half the words, then only first letters, then the whole passage.
4. With the whole passage hidden, write it (or click **Say it** and say it), then **Check**. The words you missed are underlined.

## Blurt it out

Writing down all you remember, without looking, then checking, is one of the surest ways to make reading stay.

1. Under **What to recall**, choose **This page** or this page and a few before it.
2. Click **Start**, then write (or click **Say it instead** and say) everything you remember, in any order.
3. Click **I have written all I remember**.

You see how many of the pages' key ideas you brought back, and each one you left out with the sentence of the book that holds it. Click one to go to its page. **Make cards of what I left out** turns them into flashcards.

## Questions before reading (SQ3R)

Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review: each heading of the book is turned into the question its section answers (**How do cells divide?**, **What is the cell membrane, and why does it matter?**).

1. Read a section's question, then click it to go to the section and read.
2. Without looking, write your answer under the question.
3. Click **Keep my answers as flashcards**.

It uses the book's contents, or for a PDF without them, the [headings Sidepage finds](/reading/moving-around#use-the-contents).

## Learn in order

The book's key terms, each placed after the terms it builds on: those the book brings in first and keeps using with it. A diagram shows the same with arrows. Click a term to go to where it first comes.

## Make a mnemonic

1. Write the items to remember, one per line, or select a list on the page and click **Use the selected list**.
2. Click **Make a mnemonic**.

You get the first letters as a word (**PMAT**) and a sentence whose words start with those letters. **Another sentence** makes a new one. **Keep it as a flashcard** puts it in Review.

## Past exam paper

1. Click **Choose a paper** and choose an old exam paper (a PDF or a text file).
2. Sidepage finds its numbered questions and, for each, the pages of your opened books that best answer it.
3. Click a question to see them; click a page to open the book there.

A scanned paper needs its text first: open it in Sidepage and [make it searchable](/reading/scanned-pages).

## Explain a term aloud

In the [study guide](/study/guide), under a key term, **Teach it back** asks you to explain it in your own words. Click **Explain it aloud** to say it instead of typing: it is written out on your device, then checked.

## In Review

- **Listen to my cards**: hands free, each card is read aloud, then a pause to answer in your head (choose 3 to 12 seconds), then its answer. When you listen does not change when cards come up.
- **Cards I keep forgetting**: the cards forgotten again and again after you had learned them, each with its book and page. Reading the page again often helps more than more turns of the card.

The cards made here are kept with the book's notes, so they are in [backups](/data/backup) and in [Anki decks](/study/flashcards).
