# Papers: what cites what

For a research paper (or any subject), see what it cites, what cites it and related work, as a list or a map, and open free papers straight into Sidepage. The papers come from OpenAlex, a free and open catalogue of about 250 million works.

## Explore from the paper you are reading

1. Open the paper.
2. Click **Study** in the top bar, then **Papers**.
3. Sidepage searches for the paper by its DOI or title. Pick it in **Choose the work to explore** (the right one is usually first).

You see the paper's authors, year, journal, how many times it was cited, and its abstract (click the abstract to read all of it).

## Look around it

Use the tabs under the paper:

- **Cited by**: newer work that cites it, most cited first.
- **It cites**: the works in its reference list.
- **Related**: works on the same subject.
- **Map**: the paper in the middle, what it cites in blue, what cites it in green, related work in grey. The bigger the dot, the more it has been cited. Hover over a dot to see its title.

Click any title, or any dot on the map, to explore from that paper instead. **Back** takes you to where you were.

## Open a free paper

When a paper is free to read, it shows **Free PDF**.

1. Click **Free PDF** (or **Open the free PDF** on the paper you are exploring).
2. The paper opens in Sidepage, beside your notes, and goes into your library.

Some publishers allow only browsers to download their PDFs. Sidepage then opens the PDF in your browser: save it there, then open it in Sidepage. **Its page** opens the paper's page on the publisher's site.

## Search any subject

Type a subject, a title or a DOI in the box at the top, and click **Find**.

## Searching a lot

OpenAlex is free, with a daily allowance for each network. If it says it has had enough questions for today, get a free key at [openalex.org](https://openalex.org) and paste it under **Searching a lot?** at the bottom of the panel.
