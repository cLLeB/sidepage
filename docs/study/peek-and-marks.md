# Peek, every use and study marks

Tools for reading papers and textbooks closely: see what a citation or a figure number points to without leaving the page, find every place a term comes, and mark what you know and what you still wonder about. They work in PDFs.

## Peek at citations, figures and acronyms

Rest the pointer on any of these in a PDF (on a touch screen, tap it):

| You point at | You see |
| --- | --- |
| A citation such as **[12]**, **[3, 5]** or **(Smith et al., 2020)** | Its entry in the references at the end |
| **Figure 3**, **Fig. 2a**, **Table 1** | A picture of the figure or table, with its caption |
| An acronym such as **CGM** | What it stands for, from where the text first spells it out |

Click the link in the peek to go there. To come back, click **↩ p. …** in the book's bar.

Sidepage finds the references, captions and acronyms from the PDF's own text, on your device. A scanned PDF needs to be [made searchable](/reading/scanned-pages) first.

## Where is this explained?

1. Select a word or a few words on the page.
2. Click **⋯ Tools**, then **Where is this explained?**

The sentence that says what it is shows, with its page (for example **Diffusion is the movement of…**). Click **Go there**, or **Add to notes**. When no sentence defines it, the first place it comes is shown.

## Every use in this book

1. Select a word or a few words.
2. Click **⋯ Tools**, then **Every use in this book**.

Each place the words come is listed with the words either side, lined up in a column (a concordance). Under **Order**, choose **By the word after** or **By the word before** to bring the same phrases together. Click a line to go to its page.

## How well you know each page

1. Click **⋯ Tools**.
2. Under **This page**, beside **I know it**, click **Not yet**, **Partly** or **Well**.

The page gets a red, amber or green tick on the page map at the right of the PDF. Click **Next page I do not know yet** to go through the pages marked Not yet or Partly, before an exam for instance. Click the same mark again to take it off.

**Show hard pages on the map** shades the pages much harder to read than the rest of the book (worked out from their sentences and words), so you can plan more time for them.

## Questions about a passage

1. Select the passage you have a question about.
2. Click **⋯ Tools**, then **A question about this**.
3. Write the question and click **Keep the question**.

The page gets a blue dot on the page map, and **Open questions** (the question mark) shows in the book's bar. There you can:

- click a question to go back to its passage;
- click ✨ to ask the AI, with the passage (see [Ask AI](/ai/ask-ai));
- click ✓ once you have the answer;
- click ✕ to remove it.

## Pick up the thread

Open a book you last read a few days ago, and a card shows what you were reading: **Last time, 5 days ago: pp. 40 to 46**, with the gist of those pages. Click **Go on from p. 46**, or close it.

Your marks and questions are kept with the book's notes, so they are part of [backups](/data/backup) and travel with a book you [send to another device](/data/link-devices).
